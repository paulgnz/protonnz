Every voter on XPR Network has the same problem: **how do you tell a block producer that ships from
one that just parks a domain?** The producer table on-chain gives you a URL. What that URL leads to
is up to the BP.

So we checked every one of them.

## The methodology

We took the full list of **active block producers** from the `eosio` producers table
(`is_active == 1`, 38 producers at the time of writing), and for each:

1. Fetched the URL they publish on-chain
2. Fetched `<url>/bp.json` (the standard producer manifest)
3. Visited the site itself and read what they surface — nodes, monitoring, docs, blog, community
   channels, ownership, code of conduct

The two HTTP checks are the objective bar. What's on the site itself is a judgment call.

## The technical bar: mostly clean

**37 of 38 active BPs served a valid `bp.json`** at the time of the check. One was flagged as
unreachable at the network level (a subsequent recheck showed it working — likely transient).

That's better than a lot of chains. The protocol-level BP contract on XPR — publish a URL, publish
a bp.json, run the nodes it says you run — is being met.

## The site bar: much weaker

Serving a bp.json doesn't mean the site behind it is alive. Of the 38 sites we looked at:

- Only around **4 show current 2025+ activity** — recent blog posts, real infra pages, live status.
- Roughly a third are **corporate landing pages** where the XPR BP is a footnote to another
  business (hosting company, e-commerce site, development agency).
- Several are **frozen** — the site works, but the blog stopped in 2020 or 2021.
- Two sites publish a **live node status page**.
- Two publish **chain snapshots** as a service.
- Six publish an explicit **Code of Conduct** — the standard BP page most BPs are expected to have.

That last statistic surprised us. The Code of Conduct is one of the two documents every BP is
supposed to publish (along with the ownership disclosure), and only about 15% of active BPs
actually do.

## What good BP sites do

There is one clear standout — **[cerebro.host](https://cerebro.host)** — a multi-network
infrastructure company where XPR is one chain among several. They publish per-service status
badges, Grafana dashboards, uptime figures, deploy templates, and support response times. This is
the ceiling for the field right now, and it's not XPR-dedicated.

The best XPR-focused work we saw includes:

- **[alvosec](https://alvosec.com)** — a security company running a BP. XProtect scam-domain
  scanner, a suite of free browser tools (encryption, IP checker, password generator), and
  HackerOne membership. Their free-tools-as-marketing model is exactly the shape we're aiming for
  with our own Tools dropdown, and their security focus benefits the whole network.
- **[brotonbp](https://mainnet.brotonbp.com)** — Whales Alert notification bot, a metrics dashboard
  at protonstat.info, a curated "Built on Proton" project directory.
- **[blocksforge](https://www.blocksforge.com)** — a minimal but real node status page and a
  chain-snapshots service at snapshots.blocksforge.com.
- **[saltant](https://proton.saltant.io)** — publishes hardware specs and voting commitments (the
  specs are stale, but the intent is right).

If you're a voter looking at BPs, those four plus cerebro are worth clicking through. The rest
of the field is either good enough (bp.json works, site works, minimal content) or effectively
dark.

## What we changed on our own site because of this

Auditing others is fair game only if you fix your own gaps first. Off the back of this exercise
we shipped:

- **[/status](https://www.protonnz.com/status)** — live node status page, checked from the
  visitor's browser, that pings every public endpoint every 30 seconds.
- **[/code-of-conduct](https://www.protonnz.com/code-of-conduct)** — the eight commitments we
  actually make as a BP (independence, infrastructure, security, transparency, governance,
  neutrality, communication, community).
- **[Chain Snapshots on /developers](https://www.protonnz.com/developers)** — verified,
  compressed mainnet and testnet snapshots with sha256 metadata for node operators.
- **[bp.json](https://protonnz.com/bp.json)** — cleaned up so every URL in it resolves, ownership
  disclosure points at the real page, code of conduct points at the real page, and every node is
  listed with the correct location.

None of this is revolutionary. It's the same set of pages any BP could ship in an afternoon. That
we can move to the top of the "sites that actually publish infra" list by doing this says more
about the field than about us.

## The takeaway for voters

**Vote for producers you can see shipping.** The URL in the producers table is a promise; the
site behind it is the evidence. Click through five random BPs you're voting for right now.

- Does the site load?
- Does `bp.json` resolve at the URL they publish?
- Is there any evidence of activity in the last 12 months?
- Do they say anywhere what they actually do with the block reward?
- Do they have a Code of Conduct?

Most producers pass the first two questions. Most fail one or more of the last three. That is a
signal.
