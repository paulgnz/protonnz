An active phishing campaign is draining wallets on XPR Network. It advertises a fake "$XPR
airdrop" at **xprdrop.com**, and if you connect your wallet and "claim", you unknowingly hand an
attacker a permission that lets them drain your tokens — repeatedly.

We built a free tool to fix it: **[cleanup.protonnz.com](https://cleanup.protonnz.com)**. It finds
the malicious permission and removes it in one transaction you sign yourself. It is non-custodial
and never touches your keys.

> **Two rules that stop this attack:**
> 1. Never open a link from a token memo.
> 2. There are no free XPR airdrops.

## How the scam works

This is a **permission-delegation drainer**. No private key is stolen — you authorize it yourself
by signing what looks like an airdrop claim.

1. You receive an unsolicited token (for example `XPRDROP`) in your wallet. Its memo advertises an
   airdrop site: *"Congrats! Claim your $XPR airdrop on xprdrop.com."*
2. On the site, "claiming" prompts you to sign a transaction. That transaction quietly creates a
   new permission on your account (usually named `claim`) controlled by the attacker, and links it
   to your token transfers — and sometimes to your unstaking.
3. Seconds later, the attacker uses that permission to transfer your tokens out. Because it is a
   **permission and not a one-off transfer, it persists** — they can drain you again whenever new
   funds arrive, until you delete it.

The important part: receiving the `XPRDROP` token is completely harmless. The danger is only if you
open the link and connect your wallet on the fake site.

## Am I affected?

If you ever connected your wallet to xprdrop.com (or a similar "claim your XPR" site), assume you
are affected until you check. You can verify in two ways:

- **Use our tool:** go to [cleanup.protonnz.com](https://cleanup.protonnz.com) and either connect
  your wallet or type your account name for a read-only audit. It flags any permission controlled
  by an account that isn't you.
- **Check manually:** open your account on a block explorer and look at the Keys / Auth tab. If you
  see a permission (often called `claim`) controlled by another account you don't recognise, that's
  the drainer.

**Important — if you have staked XPR:** the drainer permission is usually linked to token transfers

You do **not** need to rotate your keys — no key was stolen. Deleting the permission is the fix.

## Stay safe

Legitimate airdrops never ask you to sign an `updateauth` or `linkauth`, and never need authority
over your unstaking. When a "claim" wants permission over your account, it's a drainer. When in
doubt, don't sign — and if you've already been caught, run the cleanup and share it with anyone else
who might have been targeted.
